What is FotoBot?

Advances in large language models (LLMs) have opened new possibilities for robots to operate in open, real-world environments. As these capabilities develop towards superintelligence (SI), robots could autonomously handle more everyday tasks, giving people more time to travel, create, and share their experiences. Yet translating personal creative ideas into cultural objects through drawing, photograph, or vlog can be difficult without the necessary skills. Capturing a compelling photograph or producing a polished vlog can require professional expertise or even an entire production team.

Could SI make creative expression more accessible by enabling robots to become creative partners? Robotics research has traditionally focused on utilitarian tasks with clearly defined goals. Creative collaboration also requires robots to respond to personal intentions, aesthetic preferences, and subjective judgements. This vision motivates the FotoBot series, which explores how robots can become interactive partners in the creative process. Our mission is to help everyone bring their creative ideas to life, without requiring professional skills.

Since launching the project in 2024, we have explored several aspects of robotic photography and videography. Our work spans an LLM-integrated prototype (FotoBot), autonomous portrait photography algorithms and service workflows (LEAP), and camera following and motion control for vlog recording. Our approach combines aesthetic judgement with safe, adaptive operation in dynamic public spaces. For user interaction, we provide a comprehensive, privacy-preserving service workflow, making the image capture and store legible. It also incorporates a GPT model to parse user prompts and interpret their intent. Extensive deployment experiments have demonstrated the feasibility of operating these systems in real urban environments.

In parallel, we are exploring a general agentic photography system that uses recent advances in LLMs to interpret free-form user instructions across diverse scenarios. We are also investigating recursive self-evolving (RSI) methods to enable automatic improvement and adaptation to individual preferences. Beyond photography and vlog recording, we also plan to do robotic filming in the real-world scenarios. Through this line of work, we aim to make robotic creative assistance an efficient, reliable, and accessible part of everyday life.
